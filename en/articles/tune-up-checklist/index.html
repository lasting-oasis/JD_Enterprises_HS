What a tune-up should cover.

A tune-up is more than a quick look. Here's what a technician should check each season, and what you can do between visits.

When to book it

ENERGY STAR recommends a check-up before each season: the cooling system in spring and the heating system in fall. A heat pump does both jobs, so it gets both visits. Booking before the first hot or cold spell is easier than calling once the system is already struggling.

On every system

  • Check the thermostat settings, so the house is comfortable when you're home and saves energy when you're away.
  • Tighten electrical connections and measure the voltage and current on the motors.
  • Lubricate the moving parts that need it, to cut friction and wasted electricity.
  • Check the condensate drain, so water doesn't back up into the house or raise the humidity.
  • Run the system through a full cycle: it should start, run and shut off correctly and safely.

Cooling, in spring

  • Clean the indoor (evaporator) and outdoor (condenser) coils. Dirty coils cool less and wear the system faster.
  • Check the refrigerant charge and adjust it if needed.
  • Clean and adjust the blower, since airflow affects both comfort and efficiency.

Heating, in fall

  • Gas furnaces: check the gas connections and gas pressure, how the burners burn and the heat exchanger. Problems there affect safety as well as efficiency.
  • Heat pumps: the heating mode gets the same attention the cooling mode got in spring.

Between visits: your part

  • Check the air filter every month, especially in summer and winter. Change it when it looks dirty, and at least every three months.
  • Keep leaves, grass clippings and stored things away from the outdoor unit and the indoor equipment.
  • Keep supply vents and return grilles open and unblocked.
  • If the home has gas appliances, test the carbon monoxide alarms.

Ask the technician to show you where the filter goes and which size it takes.

After the visit

Ask for a written note of what was checked and found, and keep it. Many equipment warranties expect regular maintenance, and the notes show a pattern if a problem comes back. If a better filter comes up, the indoor air guide explains the EPA's advice on MERV ratings.

Sources

  • Maintenance Checklist, ENERGY STAR
  • Heat & Cool Efficiently, ENERGY STAR
  • Guide to Air Cleaners in the Home, U.S. Environmental Protection Agency
