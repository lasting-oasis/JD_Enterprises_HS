Reading a replacement quote.

Two quotes for the same house can describe very different jobs. Here's what a complete quote includes, so you can compare them line by line.

Sizing comes first

North Carolina's residential code says heating and cooling equipment is sized with ACCA Manual S, from a load calculation done with ACCA Manual J or another approved method. The calculation looks at the house itself: insulation, windows, air leaks and which way it faces. Ask for a copy.

A quote that simply matches the old unit's size, with no calculation, can repeat an old mistake.

The equipment, by model number

  • Model numbers for every major part: the outdoor unit, the indoor coil or air handler, and the furnace if there is one.
  • The efficiency ratings of that matched set: SEER2 for cooling, HSPF2 for a heat pump's heating and AFUE for a gas furnace.
  • An AHRI reference number. It lets you look up the rated performance of that exact combination in the AHRI Directory.

The refrigerant

New residential split systems made from January 2025 on use newer refrigerants such as R-454B or R-32. In May 2026 the EPA removed the deadline for installing R-410A equipment built before 2025, so some quotes may still offer it. Ask which refrigerant the system uses. ACCA, the contractors' trade association, has warned that R-410A prices could rise.

The work, line by line

  • Refrigerant lines: reused or replaced, and how they're tested.
  • The outdoor pad, the electrical disconnect and any wiring changes.
  • The thermostat: kept, replaced, or set up for the new system.
  • Ductwork changes, sealing or repairs.
  • The condensate drain, and for an attic unit, the drain pan and safety switch.
  • Removing the old equipment. The EPA requires refrigerant to be recovered, not vented, by a technician with Section 608 certification.
  • Start-up: the readings taken once the new system runs, written down.

Permit, license and inspection

In Mecklenburg County, installing or replacing heating and cooling equipment needs a mechanical permit from County Code Enforcement, and an inspection. The quote should say who pulls the permit.

In North Carolina, a heating contractor's license number and qualification must appear on proposals and contracts (21 NCAC 50 .0406). You can check a license with the NC State Board of Examiners of Plumbing, Heating and Fire Sprinkler Contractors.

Warranty, payment and rebates

  • The manufacturer's parts warranty: how long it lasts, and whether the equipment must be registered to get the full term.
  • The contractor's labor warranty, in writing, with what it covers.
  • A payment schedule: the deposit, when the rest is due and what the price includes.

Rebate programs change, so check current eligibility on the rebate help page. The federal 25C tax credit ended for equipment placed in service after December 31, 2025.
